# Findings: do LLM review scores track arXiv paper versions?

Status: E0/E1 complete (2026-10-21). Full statistics per model:
[`results/e0_scores_summary.md`](../results/e0_scores_summary.md) (regenerated by
`python3 src/analysis/analyze_scores.py`); per-(model, paper) rows:
[`results/e0_per_paper.jsonl`](../results/e0_per_paper.jsonl). Every number below traces to
those files or `data/processed/*.jsonl`.

## Abstract

We scored 142 arXiv versions of 50 ICLR 2024–25 submissions (26 accepted / 24 rejected;
scrubbed, anonymized, stage-labeled) with four LLM-reviewer configurations, three
repetitions each (1,359 parsed reviews). Whether an LLM reviewer can detect that a paper
improved across its revisions depends dramatically on configuration: a non-reasoning
reviewer is blind to revisions (τ ≈ 0), while reasoning-enabled reviewers track them
(τ 0.49–0.57; +0.15 to +0.28 rating points per revision step, p < 1e-4). Within one model
family (DeepSeek V4.1-Flash served behind both aliases), toggling reasoning alone moves
mean τ from −0.14 to +0.49 — the cleanest evidence that *reasoning capability, not model
family, drives revision sensitivity*. However, controlling for document length absorbs
most of the effect in every arm except Gemini text-only (+0.14/step, p = 0.001): a large
share of apparent "recognizing improvement" is "longer papers score higher". All arms are
mediocre at absolute quality (accept/reject AUC 0.66–0.71 vs 0.88 for human mean scores),
and a recognition probe shows heavy identity memorization (70% exact-title recall) but no
evidence of decision memorization.

## Setup (brief)

- **Data**: 50 ICLR 2024–25 papers matched to arXiv version chains (all ≥2 versions,
  stratified accepted/rejected × 2 vs 3+ versions), 142 obtainable versions converted from
  LaTeX source to anonymized markdown (authors, acknowledgments, venue strings, camera-ready
  toggles, repo links, affiliation lines removed; audit in `data/processed/scrub_log.jsonl`).
  Each version stage-labeled (pre_submission / during_review / post_decision) against the
  official ICLR timeline of its year.
- **Rubric**: ICLR-style JSON review (summary, strengths/weaknesses, soundness /
  presentation / contribution 1–4, confidence 1–5, accept bool) with the overall rating
  restricted to the realized human scale {1,3,5,6,8,10}.
- **Arms** (K=3 reps, temperature 1.0, blinded independent calls in shuffled order):
  `deepseek-chat` (serves `deepseek-flash`, reportedly V4.1-Flash — reasoning OFF; 10-paper
  pilot only), `deepseek-reasoner` (same served model — reasoning ON; 50 papers),
  `gemini-3.8-flash` text-only and text+figures (reasoning on by default; 50 papers;
  figures = the version's own referenced images, ≤20, identity-filtered).

## Findings

### 1. Version tracking is configuration-dependent — and reasoning is the hinge

| arm | reasoning | papers | mean τ (bootstrap 95% CI) | raw pts/revision (mixedlm) | ICC(2,1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| deepseek-chat | off | 10 | −0.14 [−0.65, +0.37] | +0.01 (p=0.90) | 0.26 |
| deepseek-reasoner | on | 50 | +0.49 [+0.30, +0.67] | +0.15 (p<1e-4) | 0.64 |
| gemini-3.8-flash (text) | on | 50 | +0.57 [+0.35, +0.77] | +0.28 (p<1e-4) | 0.81 |
| gemini-3.8-flash (+figs) | on | 50 | +0.48 [+0.22, +0.72] | +0.22 (p<1e-4) | 0.81 |

Page's trend test agrees (p = 0.02–0.04 for the three reasoning arms; p = 0.78 for chat).
Same prompts, same texts, same scale. The deepseek pair is the key contrast: **the same
served model with reasoning toggled goes from blind to tracking** (caveat: the
reasoning-off arm is pilot-sized, n=10 papers; extending it to 50 is one command).

### 2. Verbosity bias absorbs most of the effect

Adding log(document words) to the mixed model (`rating ~ version_position + log_words +
(1|paper)`):

| arm | version coef after control | log_words coef | survives? |
|---|---|---|---|
| deepseek-chat | −0.05 (p=0.66) | +1.56 (p=0.03) | null stays null |
| deepseek-reasoner | +0.07 (p=0.10) | +1.14 (p=0.0004) | **no** |
| gemini text | **+0.14 (p=0.001)** | +1.95 (p<1e-4) | **yes** |
| gemini +figs | +0.06 (p=0.18) | +2.27 (p<1e-4) | **no** |

Later versions are longer, and longer papers score higher. Only Gemini-text retains an
independent version effect. Two readings, not mutually exclusive: (a) LLM reviewers partly
reward length itself (classic verbosity bias); (b) real revisions genuinely add content,
so length is a *mediator*, not only a confound — distinguishing these needs E4's synthetic
probes (length-matched edits).

### 3. Placebo behavior: scores move on near-identical input

On the 17 TRUE-placebo pairs (cosmetic text churn AND unchanged figures), score deltas
should be zero. Share exactly zero: Gemini arms ≈ 47–53%, reasoner 24%, chat 40%; mean
|delta| 0.31 (Gemini) to 0.53 (reasoner) rating points. This is each arm's noise floor —
revision-sized claims below it are not interpretable. Reasoner is notably the *noisiest*
on placebo while being directionally right on major revisions (64% up / 14% down).

### 4. Longitudinal sensitivity ≠ cross-sectional validity

Accept/reject discrimination at the anchor version (latest pre-decision): AUC 0.69–0.71
for every reasoning arm (human mean score as reference: 0.88); correlation with human mean
scores 0.16–0.28 at 50 papers (the 10-paper pilot's 0.7+ was small-sample optimism).
**The reviewers can see a paper improving while barely separating good papers from bad
ones.** For deployment-style questions ("can this replace a reviewer's score?") the
cross-sectional number is the relevant one, and it is weak.

### 5. Scale compression in every arm

No arm uses 1, 8 is rare, 10 never appears; 1/3 of papers get identical Gemini scores
across all versions. DeepSeek-chat is also harsh (mean 4.2 vs human 6.0); Gemini sits near
the human mean but compressed. Score-distribution mismatch alone makes raw-score deployment
comparisons misleading.

## E4: length-matched edit probes (deepseek-reasoner arm; Gemini arm pending)

25 papers × 6 variants (base / three length-preserved degradations / +31% filler /
−40% appendix-cut), K=3, 444/444 parsed. Full tables:
[`results/e4_probes_summary.md`](../results/e4_probes_summary.md).

| variant (direction, length) | paired Δ vs base | p (mixed model) | right subscore hit? |
|---|---|---|---|
| typos (quality↓, len=) | **−0.41** | 0.003 | yes — presentation −0.77 |
| corrupt_numbers (soundness↓, len=) | −0.27 | 0.053 | **no** — presentation moved, not soundness |
| shuffle_paragraphs (coherence↓, len=) | **+0.29** | 0.034 | — (presentation −0.37 yet rating UP) |
| pad_filler (content=, len **+31%**) | **−0.92** | <1e-4 | — (presentation −1.29) |
| drop_appendix (core=, len **−40%**) | −0.01 | 0.95 | — |

Interpretation:
1. **The observational length effect is mediation, not verbosity bias.** Experimentally
   *adding* 30% content-free text tanks the score (−0.92) and *removing* 40% of the
   document (appendix) changes nothing. The reviewer does not reward length per se — in
   real revisions, length grows because content grows, and that is what the E1 log-words
   covariate was absorbing.
2. **Surface noise is detected; structural and numerical damage is not.** Typos are
   correctly punished on presentation. But corrupting result numbers (creating internal
   inconsistencies) is only marginally penalized — and misattributed to presentation — and
   **shuffling paragraph order *raises* the rating** (+0.29, p=0.03) while presentation
   drops: the reviewer reads local fluency, not document-level argument structure.
3. By the pre-registered mechanical rule the reasoner is **neither content-sensitive nor
   length-sensitive** at these thresholds: its probe responses are selective (fluff,
   typos) but miss the manipulations a competent human reviewer must catch.

## Contamination / recognition probe

Run 2026-10-21, `python3 src/review/run_recognition_probe.py --which anchor`:
`deepseek-chat`, temperature 0, one anchor version per paper (41 during_review + 9
pre_submission), 50 calls, 50/50 parsed_ok, ~$0.23. Log:
`data/processed/recognition_log.jsonl`.

- **Recognition claim rate: 38/50 (76%)**.
- Among the 38 claims: **title correct 35/38 (92%)** (70% of all anchors), **≥1 author
  surname correct 18/38 (47%)**, venue correct 19/38 (50%), decision correct 15/38 (39%) —
  but split by truth: **accepted 15/21 (71%) vs rejected 0/17 (0%)**; every non-null
  decision guess was "accepted" (20 guesses, 18 null), so decision "knowledge" behaves like
  an accept-biased prior, not memory of outcomes.
- Mean self-reported confidence: 0.655 on claims vs 0.088 on non-claims.

Takeaway: identity contamination is heavy — despite scrubbing, the model recovers the exact
title for most pilot papers and at least one author for about half of its claims — while
there is no evidence it remembers ICLR decisions (0% correct on rejected papers). So the
version-tracking signal cannot be read as "the model remembers which version was accepted",
but authorship/title memorization means its scores are not blind to paper identity either.

Grading audit: placebo-subset ns in the report hand-recounted (deepseek-chat 5,
gemini arms 17 — all match) and probe grading re-derived on 3 random log rows against the
manifest/ICLR ground truth (all match).

## Threats to validity

1. **Text-only extraction misses figure-only revisions** — 74% of adjacent pairs change
   figures; the +figs arm mitigates but uses converted stills, not the compiled PDF.
2. **Length confound** (finding 2) — needs E4 length-matched synthetic edits to separate
   mediator from bias.
3. **Contamination** — identity (title/author) memorization is demonstrated; post-cutoff
   papers and the pre/post-cutoff split are the designed control, not yet run.
4. **DeepSeek-chat arm is pilot-sized** (10 papers) and the reasoning contrast relies on
   third-party reports that both DeepSeek aliases serve V4.1-Flash (response `model` field:
   `deepseek-flash` for both; verify against DeepSeek's changelog before publication).
5. **Selection bias**: rejected papers that post to arXiv (and revise) are not typical
   rejected papers; match rates differ by outcome (76–83% accepted vs 37–50% rejected).
6. **Single rubric, single prompt, temperature 1.0** — prompt-sensitivity sweep not yet run.
7. Institution names in running prose are audited but not removed (e.g. "labelers from
   KAIST"); affiliation-leak severity is bounded by the recognition probe.
