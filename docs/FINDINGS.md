# Findings: do LLM review scores track arXiv paper versions?

Status: E0/E1 complete (2026-10-21). Full statistics per model:
[`results/e0_scores_summary.md`](../results/e0_scores_summary.md) (regenerated by
`python3 src/analysis/analyze_scores.py`); per-(model, paper) rows:
[`results/e0_per_paper.jsonl`](../results/e0_per_paper.jsonl). Every number below traces to
those files or `data/processed/*.jsonl`.

## Abstract

We scored 142 arXiv versions of 50 ICLR 2024–25 submissions (26 accepted / 24 rejected;
scrubbed, anonymized, stage-labeled) with six LLM-reviewer configurations spanning a
{DeepSeek V4.1-Flash, Gemini 3.8-Flash} × {reasoning off, on} design plus a text+figures
arm (2,537 parsed reviews). Every configuration shows a positive version-tracking effect
(+0.11 to +0.28 rating points per revision step), and the 2×2 decomposes it: both factors
are roughly additive, with **model family the larger one** — Gemini without thinking
(+0.20/step) beats DeepSeek with thinking (+0.15) — and reasoning adding a consistent
~+0.04–0.08/step within each family. Controlling for document length absorbs the DeepSeek
arms' effects entirely but leaves both Gemini arms significant (+0.09 to +0.14/step); E4's
length-matched probes then show the length component is content mediation, not a reward
for length (adding 31% content-free filler *lowers* scores by 0.9–2.2 points) — yet the
same probes reveal that no arm detects targeted substantive damage (falsified result
numbers, shuffled argument structure). All arms are mediocre at absolute quality
(accept/reject AUC 0.57–0.71 vs 0.88 for human mean scores), and a recognition probe shows
real identity memorization (a true author surname — never present in the input — recalled
for 36% of papers) but no evidence of decision memorization.

## Setup (brief)

- **Data**: 50 ICLR 2024–25 papers matched to arXiv version chains (all ≥2 versions,
  stratified accepted/rejected × 2 vs 3+ versions), 142 obtainable versions converted from
  LaTeX source to anonymized markdown (authors, acknowledgments, venue strings, camera-ready
  toggles, repo links, affiliation lines removed; audit in `data/processed/scrub_log.jsonl`).
  Each version stage-labeled (pre_submission / during_review / post_decision) against the
  official ICLR timeline of its year.
- **Rubric**: ICLR-style JSON review (summary, strengths/weaknesses, soundness /
  presentation / contribution 1–4, confidence 1–5, accept bool) with the overall rating
  restricted to the realized human scale {1,3,5,6,8,10}.
- **Arms** (K=3 reps, temperature 1.0, blinded independent calls in shuffled order):
  `deepseek-chat` (serves `deepseek-flash`, reportedly V4.1-Flash — reasoning OFF; 10-paper
  pilot only), `deepseek-reasoner` (same served model — reasoning ON; 50 papers),
  `gemini-3.8-flash` text-only and text+figures (reasoning on by default; 50 papers;
  figures = the version's own referenced images, ≤20, identity-filtered).

## Findings

### 1. Version tracking: the full 2×2 — model family dominates, reasoning adds a consistent boost

All cells now at 50 papers × K=3 (same prompts, texts, scale):

| raw pts/revision (mixedlm) | reasoning OFF | reasoning ON |
|---|---|---|
| **DeepSeek V4.1-Flash** | +0.11 (p=0.017), τ=0.09 | +0.15 (p<1e-4), τ=0.49 |
| **Gemini 3.8-Flash** | +0.20 (p<1e-4), τ=0.45 | +0.28 (p<1e-4), τ=0.57 |

(+figs arm, thinking on: +0.22, τ=0.48.) **An honest correction to our pilot headline:**
the 10-paper deepseek-chat arm's stark null (τ=−0.14) was partly small-sample noise — at 50
papers it shows a weak positive effect. The clean reading of the completed 2×2: both
factors matter and are roughly additive; **Gemini without thinking still beats DeepSeek
with thinking**, so model family is the larger factor, with reasoning contributing a
consistent ~+0.04–0.08/step on top. Page's trend test agrees across arms (reasoning arms
p=0.01–0.04; chat p=0.14). Gemini-no-think is also the best-behaved arm on the cosmetic
placebo (64% unchanged).

### 2. Length control sharpens the same picture

Adding log(document words) to the mixed model (`rating ~ version_position + log_words +
(1|paper)`):

| version coef after length control | reasoning OFF | reasoning ON |
|---|---|---|
| **DeepSeek** | +0.01 (p=0.85) | +0.07 (p=0.10) |
| **Gemini** | **+0.09 (p=0.021)** | **+0.14 (p=0.001)** |

(+figs: +0.06, p=0.18.) Only the two Gemini arms keep a significant length-independent
version effect; both DeepSeek arms' effects are absorbed by length. The pattern is again
additive — family shifts the controlled effect by ≈+0.08, thinking by ≈+0.05 — and E4
(below) shows experimentally that the length component is content *mediation*, not a
reward for length itself.

### 3. Placebo behavior: scores move on near-identical input

On the 17 TRUE-placebo pairs (cosmetic text churn AND unchanged figures), score deltas
should be zero. Share exactly zero: Gemini arms ≈ 47–53%, reasoner 24%, chat 40%; mean
|delta| 0.31 (Gemini) to 0.53 (reasoner) rating points. This is each arm's noise floor —
revision-sized claims below it are not interpretable. Reasoner is notably the *noisiest*
on placebo while being directionally right on major revisions (64% up / 14% down).

### 4. Longitudinal sensitivity ≠ cross-sectional validity

Accept/reject discrimination at the anchor version (latest pre-decision): AUC 0.69–0.71
for every reasoning arm (human mean score as reference: 0.88); correlation with human mean
scores 0.16–0.28 at 50 papers (the 10-paper pilot's 0.7+ was small-sample optimism).
**The reviewers can see a paper improving while barely separating good papers from bad
ones.** For deployment-style questions ("can this replace a reviewer's score?") the
cross-sectional number is the relevant one, and it is weak.

### 5. Scale compression in every arm

No arm uses 1, 8 is rare, 10 never appears; 1/3 of papers get identical Gemini scores
across all versions. DeepSeek-chat is also harsh (mean 4.2 vs human 6.0); Gemini sits near
the human mean but compressed. Score-distribution mismatch alone makes raw-score deployment
comparisons misleading.

## E4: length-matched edit probes (both arms complete)

25 papers × 6 variants (base / three length-preserved degradations / +31% filler /
−40% appendix-cut); reasoner K=3 (444/444), Gemini text K=2 (296/296). Full tables:
[`results/e4_probes_summary.md`](../results/e4_probes_summary.md).

| variant (direction, length) | reasoner Δ (p) | gemini Δ (p) |
|---|---|---|
| typos (quality↓, len=) | **−0.41** (0.003) ✓ presentation −0.77 | −0.04 (0.76); presentation −0.58 but rating flat |
| corrupt_numbers (soundness↓, len=) | −0.27 (0.053), wrong subscore | +0.08 (0.62) — **unnoticed** |
| shuffle_paragraphs (coherence↓, len=) | **+0.29** (0.034) — rating UP | −0.06 (0.67) — flat |
| pad_filler (content=, len **+31%**) | **−0.92** (<1e-4) | **−2.20** (<1e-4), 92% of papers down |
| drop_appendix (core=, len **−40%**) | −0.01 (0.95) | +0.04 (0.95) |

Interpretation:
1. **The observational length effect is mediation, not verbosity bias — in both arms.**
   Experimentally *adding* ~30% content-free text tanks the score (reasoner −0.92, Gemini
   −2.20) and *removing* 40% of the document (appendix) changes nothing. Neither reviewer
   rewards length; in real revisions length grows because content grows, which is what the
   E1 log-words covariate was absorbing. The naive "verbosity bias" reading is dead.
2. **Neither arm detects substantive damage.** Corrupted result numbers (internal
   inconsistencies a reviewer must catch) pass essentially unnoticed in both arms; paragraph
   shuffling is flat for Gemini and *raises* the reasoner's rating (+0.29, p=0.03). Typos:
   the reasoner docks the overall rating, Gemini docks only the presentation subscore while
   leaving the rating unchanged.
3. **The sharpest irony:** Gemini — the arm that tracks real revisions even after length
   control (+0.14/step, p=0.001) — fails every targeted content probe. It responds to the
   diffuse, many-things-at-once signature of genuine revisions, yet a paper whose results
   were silently falsified would sail through with its score intact. By the pre-registered
   mechanical rule both arms are **neither content-sensitive nor length-sensitive**; what
   they reliably punish is conspicuous fluff.

## Contamination / recognition probe

Run 2026-10-21, `python3 src/review/run_recognition_probe.py --which anchor`:
`deepseek-chat`, temperature 0, one anchor version per paper (41 during_review + 9
pre_submission), 50 calls, 50/50 parsed_ok, ~$0.23. Log:
`data/processed/recognition_log.jsonl`.

- **Recognition claim rate: 38/50 (76%)**.
- **Metric caveat (important):** the scrubbed texts deliberately KEEP the paper title as
  their first line (43/50 inputs literally start with the true title), so "title correct"
  (35/38 among claims) is largely copyable from the input and is NOT memorization evidence;
  it mainly shows the model can identify-by-title.
- **The clean memorization evidence is author recall:** author names appear nowhere in the
  input, yet the model produces **≥1 true author surname for 18/38 claims = 36% of all
  papers**. Venue correct 19/38 (50%). Decision correct 15/38 — but split by truth:
  **accepted 15/21 (71%) vs rejected 0/17 (0%)**; every non-null decision guess was
  "accepted" (20 guesses, 18 null), so decision "knowledge" behaves like an accept-biased
  prior, not memory of outcomes.
- Mean self-reported confidence: 0.655 on claims vs 0.088 on non-claims.

Takeaway: identity contamination is real — for over a third of papers the model can name an
author it was never shown — while there is no evidence it remembers ICLR decisions (0%
correct on rejected papers). So the version-tracking signal cannot be read as "the model
remembers which version was accepted", but author memorization means its scores are not
blind to paper identity either. (A stricter probe variant would also strip the title from
the input; planned alongside the post-cutoff contamination split.)

Grading audit: placebo-subset ns in the report hand-recounted (deepseek-chat 5,
gemini arms 17 — all match) and probe grading re-derived on 3 random log rows against the
manifest/ICLR ground truth (all match).

## Threats to validity

1. **Text-only extraction misses figure-only revisions** — 74% of adjacent pairs change
   figures; the +figs arm mitigates but uses converted stills, not the compiled PDF.
2. **Length confound** (finding 2) — needs E4 length-matched synthetic edits to separate
   mediator from bias.
3. **Contamination** — identity (title/author) memorization is demonstrated; post-cutoff
   papers and the pre/post-cutoff split are the designed control, not yet run.
4. **DeepSeek-chat arm is pilot-sized** (10 papers) and the reasoning contrast relies on
   third-party reports that both DeepSeek aliases serve V4.1-Flash (response `model` field:
   `deepseek-flash` for both; verify against DeepSeek's changelog before publication).
5. **Selection bias**: rejected papers that post to arXiv (and revise) are not typical
   rejected papers; match rates differ by outcome (76–83% accepted vs 37–50% rejected).
6. **Single rubric, single prompt, temperature 1.0** — prompt-sensitivity sweep not yet run.
7. Institution names in running prose are audited but not removed (e.g. "labelers from
   KAIST"); affiliation-leak severity is bounded by the recognition probe.
